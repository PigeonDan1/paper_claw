### 1. Improving the Clinical Generalizability of Machine Learning-Based Alzheimer's Disease Detection Across Domains and Modalities: A Comprehensive Review

👤 **作者**: Yingchao Huang, Yuhan Su, Xin Wang, Shanshan Yao
🔗 **来源**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08188v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08188v1)

**摘要**
> Objective: This review aims to provide a comprehensive synthesis of domain adaptation (DA) and domain general- ization (DG) strategies for AD detection. It focuses on their potential to improve the robustness, generalizability, and clinical reliability of ML-based diagnostic systems. Methods: We conduct a comprehensive scoping review of existing DA and DG approaches across multiple data modalities. The reviewed studies are organized according to data modality, algorithmic framework, adaptation strat- egy, clinical relevance, and reported cross-domain performance. Key methodological trends, performance gains, limitations, and remaining challenges are analyzed to identify future research directions. Results: DA and DG approaches have demonstrated encouraging improvements in cross-domain performance and model robustness. Emerging modalities, such as speech-based approaches, EEG and eye-tracking data, show great po- tential for early, accessible, and non-invasive cognitive assessments. However, important limitations remain, including insufficient external validation, limited interpretability, computational complexity, privacy concerns, and challenges in scaling to routine clinical practice. Conclusion: DA and DG provide important methodological pathways for developing more generalizable ML-based systems for AD detection. Future research should prioritize explainable, computationally efficient, privacy-preserving, and clinically validated DA/DG techniques. Strengthening these aspects will be essential for translating robust AI- based AD detection tools into clinically deployable solutions.

### 2. VoxCanvas-41M: A Large-Scale Bilingual Dataset for Instruction-Based Speech Editing and Its Learning Dynamics

👤 **作者**: Zhikang Niu, Wenming Tu, Yalin Li, Yanru Huo, Junxi Liu, Tianrui Wang, Ruiqi Yan, Junxuan Liang, Ziyang Ma, Zilong Zheng, Xie Chen
🔗 **来源**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08251v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08251v1)

**摘要**
> Recent advances in speech generation have spurred the development of speech editing techniques. However, achieving high-quality speech editing remains challenging due to two key limitations. First, the major hurdle is the lack of large-scale, high-quality speech editing datasets. Constructing such datasets is costly, while open-source, scalable data pipelines remain limited. Second, the learning dynamics of speech editing models remain poorly understood. In particular, how models acquire editing capabilities and the relationship between generation and editing remain unclear. To address these challenges, we introduce VoxCanvas-41M, a large-scale bilingual dataset comprising approximately 41 million speech editing pairs. VoxCanvas-41M covers two major categories: content editing, including single-span, multi-span, and location-reference editing, and paralinguistic editing, covering the insertion and deletion of 39 classes of nonverbal events such as laughter, sighs, and coughs. Building on VoxCanvas-41M, we train VoxCanvas-Edit, an instruction-based speech editing model, demonstrating the effectiveness of our dataset in improving editing performance. Furthermore, we systematically investigate the learning dynamics of speech editing, examining how models learn different editing operations and how data scale, task interactions, and training strategies affect editing performance. The dataset, pipeline, and demos are publicly available.
